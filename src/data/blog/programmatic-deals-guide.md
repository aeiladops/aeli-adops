As digital publishing evolved from manual insertion orders to automated buying, programmatic transactions expanded far beyond open auctions. Today, publishers can package their premium inventory into specialized programmatic deals that combine automated workflow efficiency with guaranteed pricing and buyer relationships.

The three primary programmatic deal types are **Programmatic Guaranteed (PG)**, **Preferred Deals (PD)**, and **Private Marketplace (PMP)** auctions. Understanding their mechanics allows publishers to capture maximum advertiser value.

  <span class="callout-label">Key Takeaway</span>
  <p>Programmatic deals allow publishers to bypass open market commodity pricing by packaging premium audience segments, high-viewability inventory, and custom ad formats directly for targeted buyers.</p>
</div>

### Programmatic Deal Types Comparison

![Programmatic Deal Guidebook](/images/blog/programmatic-deal-guidebook.png)

| Feature | Programmatic Guaranteed (PG) | Preferred Deals (PD) | Private Marketplace (PMP) | Open Auction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inventory Commitment** | Guaranteed volume commitment | Non-guaranteed | Non-guaranteed | Non-guaranteed |
| **Pricing Mechanism** | Fixed CPM price | Fixed CPM price | Auction with Floor Price | Open Dynamic Auction |
| **Auction Priority** | High (Equal to Direct) | First look before open auction | Priority invite-only auction | Lowest priority tier |
| **Buyer Competition** | 1-to-1 Single buyer | 1-to-1 Single buyer | 1-to-Few Selected buyers | Open to all DSP buyers |
| **Operational Overhead** | Moderate setup requirement | Low setup requirement | Low to Moderate | Fully Automated |

### 1. Programmatic Guaranteed (PG)

In a Programmatic Guaranteed transaction, the publisher and buyer negotiate a fixed CPM price and a guaranteed impression volume for a specific timeframe. The deal executes programmatically via DSP/SSP integration, eliminating manual billing and tags.

- **How it Works**: The buyer commits to purchasing 100% of the targeted impressions matching the deal criteria, and the publisher's ad server reserves that volume ahead of open auction demand.
- **Pricing**: Premium fixed CPM (often $10.00 – $35.00+ depending on audience and format).
- **Best Used For**: Agency buyers wanting direct-sold certainty without manual PDF insertion orders.

### 2. Preferred Deals (PD)

Preferred Deals (also known as "Unreserved Fixed Price" deals) give a single buyer a "first look" at specified inventory at a pre-negotiated fixed CPM price before that inventory is made available to open auctions.

- **How it Works**: When an ad request occurs, the ad server checks if the Preferred Deal buyer wants the impression at the agreed CPM. If the buyer passes, the impression falls through to header bidding and open auctions.
- **Pricing**: Fixed agreed CPM, but no volume commitment from the buyer.
- **Best Used For**: Monetizing high-value context or specific geo-segments where buyers want priority access but cannot commit to rigid volume quotas.

<div class="publisher-callout publisher-tip">
  <span class="callout-label">Publisher Tip</span>
  <p>Monitor Preferred Deal bid response rates closely. If a buyer accepts less than 5% of offered impressions, review targeting parameters or renegotiate the floor price to improve transaction efficiency.</p>
</div>

### 3. Private Marketplace (PMP)

A Private Marketplace (PMP) is an invite-only programmatic auction where a publisher offers a selected group of buyers exclusive access to premium inventory before open auction bidding begins.

- **How it Works**: The publisher creates a Deal ID in their SSP/GAM platform with a designated floor price. Invited buyers bid against each other in a private mini-auction. The highest bidder above the floor wins the impression.
- **Pricing**: Floor price with dynamic auction competition.
- **Best Used For**: Retaining pricing power while fostering healthy buyer competition across curated content channels (e.g., Finance, Tech, Auto).

### Selecting the Right Deal Strategy

A mature publisher ad stack utilizes all three models strategically:
1. Secure baseline revenue with **Programmatic Guaranteed** for premium direct buyers.
2. Capture buyer first-look demand with **Preferred Deals**.
3. Elevate floor prices across category verticals using targeted **Private Marketplaces**.
4. Allow remaining unreserved inventory to clear through optimized **Open Auctions**.

By offering buyers flexible programmatic transaction types, publishers build stronger demand relationships and command premium yield across all channels.
